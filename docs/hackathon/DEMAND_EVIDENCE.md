## Product hypotheses to validate

Thin markets can exhibit price impact, variable execution during volatility, fragmented liquidity and a difficult transition to sustainable secondary trading. RetroPick's initial hypothesis is that transparent bonding discovery followed by Kuru limit-order trading addresses part of that lifecycle.

These are qualitative observations and product hypotheses, not documented interview findings. Kuru provides explicit limit prices and orderbook workflows; it does not guarantee deep liquidity, remove slippage or outperform every AMM. Outcomes depend on depth, spreads, volatility and participants.

## What the deployed evidence proves

The Monad Testnet candidate demonstrates issuance, bonding, graduation, a Kuru market and liquidity vault, protected custody, and a two-actor order/fill/cancel sequence. These were functional test transactions. They do not establish organic trading volume, retained users, commercial demand or revenue.
